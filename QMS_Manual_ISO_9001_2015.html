1.0Scope

1.1General

1.1.1 This manual describes the Quality Management System (QMS) established, documented, implemented and maintained by High Country to demonstrate its ability to consistently provide products and services that meet customer, statutory and regulatory requirements, and to enhance customer satisfaction through the effective application of the system, including processes for improvement and the assurance of conformity.

1.1.2 The QMS described in this manual conforms to ISO 9001:2015, Quality management systems — Requirements.

1.1.3 The QMS also takes into account the applicable requirements and guidance of the American Institute of Architects (AIA), the Architectural Woodwork Institute (AWI), and Underwriters Laboratories (UL), as referenced in Section 2.

1.2.3 The scope of the QMS is maintained as documented information and is made available to interested parties on request.

1.3Application and Justification of Non-Applicable Requirements

1.3.1 ISO 9001:2015 does not permit "exclusions" in the sense used by earlier editions of the standard. Where High Country determines that a requirement of the standard cannot be applied, that determination is justified in this section. A requirement may be determined not applicable only where it does not affect the ability or responsibility of High Country to ensure the conformity of its products and services or to enhance customer satisfaction.

1.3.2 Processes that are applicable but are performed by external providers do not qualify as non-applicable. Such processes are controlled in accordance with Section 8.4, Control of Externally Provided Processes, Products and Services.

1.3.3 The Quality Assurance Manager is responsible for identifying any requirement considered not applicable and for presenting the justification to top management. Top management evaluates and approves such determinations within the framework of the management review (Section 9.3 and QOP-56-01, Management Review).

1.3.4 DETERMINATIONS OF NON-APPLICABILITY:

None. All requirements of ISO 9001:2015 are applicable to the High Country Quality Management System.

1.4Purpose of This Manual

1.4.1 The purpose of this manual is to:

  • Define and describe the Quality Management System and its scope;

  • Define the interaction and sequence of the processes that make up the QMS;

  • Define the roles, responsibilities and authorities of personnel involved in the operation of the system;

  • Reference the operational procedures, work instructions and forms that provide the detailed methods; and

  • Inform customers, external providers and other interested parties of the controls implemented at High Country to assure quality.

1.4.2 ISO 9001:2015 does not require a quality manual. High Country maintains this manual as documented information because it is useful for training, for customer and third-party assessment, and for defining the interaction of processes as required by Clause 4.4.

4.4Quality Management System and Its Processesexpanded in 2015

4.4.1Process approach

4.4.1.1 The QMS is established, implemented, maintained and continually improved as a system of interrelated processes. All main activities of the system are defined as Quality System Processes (QSPs) and are grouped into the following four categories, organized into a Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle:

  • Operational Processes (formerly Product Realization Processes) — Section 8

  • Performance Evaluation and Improvement Processes — Sections 9 and 10

  • Leadership and Planning Processes — Sections 5 and 6

  • Support Processes — Section 7

4.4.1.2 For each QSP, High Country determines:

  • The required inputs and the expected outputs;

  • The sequence and interaction of the process with other processes;

  • The criteria and methods, including monitoring, measurement and related performance indicators, needed to ensure effective operation and control;

  • The resources needed and their availability;

  • The assignment of responsibilities and authorities (process owners);

  • The risks and opportunities determined in accordance with Section 6.1, and the actions planned to address them;

  • The methods for monitoring, measuring and evaluating the process and, if needed, the changes required to ensure it achieves its intended results; and

  • The opportunities for improvement of the process and of the QMS.

4.4.1.3 The sequence and interaction of the QSPs are illustrated in the QMS Process Map and defined in detail in the Quality System Process Matrix at Appendix A. Each QSP is further documented in this manual and in the associated operational procedures and work instructions.

5.1.1General

5.1.1.1 Top management demonstrates leadership and commitment with respect to the QMS by:

  • Taking accountability for the effectiveness of the QMS;

  • Ensuring that the quality policy and quality objectives are established for the QMS and are compatible with the context and strategic direction of High Country;

  • Ensuring the integration of the QMS requirements into the business processes of High Country;

  • Promoting the use of the process approach and risk-based thinking;

  • Ensuring that the resources needed for the QMS are available;

  • Communicating the importance of effective quality management and of conforming to the QMS requirements;

  • Ensuring that the QMS achieves its intended results;

  • Engaging, directing and supporting persons to contribute to the effectiveness of the QMS;

  • Promoting improvement; and

  • Supporting other relevant management roles to demonstrate their leadership as it applies to their areas of responsibility.

5.1.1.2 Accountability for the effectiveness of the QMS rests with top management and is not delegated. This is a change from the previous revision of this manual, in which certain of these duties were assigned to a Management Representative.

5.1.2Customer focus

5.1.2.1 Top management demonstrates leadership and commitment with respect to customer focus by ensuring that:

  • Customer requirements and applicable statutory and regulatory requirements are determined, understood and consistently met — through the process of order and contract review defined in Section 8.2;

  • The risks and opportunities that can affect conformity of products and services and the ability to enhance customer satisfaction are determined and addressed — in accordance with Section 6.1; and

  • The focus on enhancing customer satisfaction is maintained — through the monitoring defined in Section 9.1.2.

5.1.2.2 Top management ensures that customer requirements are met through verification of products and services at the appropriate stages, as defined in Sections 8.5, 8.6 and 9.1.

6.0Planning

6.1Actions to Address Risks and Opportunitiesnew in 2015

6.1.1Determining risks and opportunities

6.1.1.1 When planning the QMS, High Country considers the issues determined under Section 4.1 and the requirements determined under Section 4.2, and determines the risks and opportunities that need to be addressed in order to:

  • Give assurance that the QMS can achieve its intended results;

  • Enhance desirable effects;

  • Prevent, or reduce, undesired effects; and

  • Achieve improvement.

6.1.1.2 Risk-based thinking replaces the separate requirement for preventive action contained in earlier editions of ISO 9001. The determination of risks and opportunities described in this section is the mechanism by which High Country now satisfies the intent of preventive action. QOP-85-03 has been retained and its scope adjusted accordingly; preventive action requests raised under that procedure feed the risk register maintained under this section.

6.1.2Planning actions

6.1.2.1 High Country plans the actions to address the determined risks and opportunities, how to integrate and implement those actions into its QMS processes (Section 4.4), and how to evaluate the effectiveness of those actions.

6.1.2.2 Actions taken are proportionate to the potential effect on the conformity of products and services. Options to address risk include avoiding the risk, taking the risk in order to pursue an opportunity, eliminating the risk source, changing the likelihood or consequences, sharing the risk, or accepting the risk by informed decision. Opportunities can lead to the adoption of new practices, launching of new products, opening of new markets, addressing new customers, building partnerships, using new technology, or other desirable and feasible possibilities.

6.1.2.3 Risks and opportunities are identified at the process level by the process owners and at the system level by top management. They are recorded in the Risk and Opportunity Register maintained by the Quality Assurance Manager. Each entry records the process affected, the risk or opportunity, an assessment of likelihood and consequence, the planned action, the responsible person, the target date, and the evaluation of effectiveness.

6.1.2.4 The register is reviewed at each management review (Section 9.3) and is updated whenever a change of context, a significant nonconformity, a customer complaint, a change in the organization, or a new or revised statutory or regulatory requirement makes it necessary.

6.1.2.5 Risks and opportunities are also considered as part of order and contract review (Section 8.2.3), design and development planning (Section 8.3.2), external provider evaluation (Section 8.4.1) and the planning of changes (Sections 6.3 and 8.5.6).

Note: ISO 9001:2015 does not require a documented risk management method or a formal risk register. High Country has chosen to maintain one because it provides objective evidence of risk-based thinking for audit purposes and supports the analysis required under Section 9.1.3.

7.0Support

Resources include personnel, infrastructure, process environment, monitoring and measuring resources, organizational knowledge, materials, information and financial resources.

7.1.1.2 In determining resources, High Country considers the capabilities of, and constraints on, existing internal resources, and what needs to be obtained from external providers.

It may take the form of personnel assignments, allocation of space or equipment, training, procurement decisions or budgets. Depending on the nature of the activity, resource requirements are defined in this manual and the operational procedures (QOP-42-01), in product and process drawings and specifications (QOP-71-01, QOP-73-01), in production plans (QOP-75-01), in job descriptions, competence matrices and training programs (QOP-62-01), and in the records of management review, quality objectives and corrective action (QOP-56-01, QOP-85-03).

7.1.5Monitoring and measuring resources

7.1.5.1 General. High Country determines and provides the resources needed to ensure valid and reliable results when monitoring or measuring is used to verify the conformity of products and services. Appropriate documented information is retained as evidence of fitness for purpose.

7.1.5.2 The scope of the control system extends to measuring and test equipment, comparative reference hardware such as gauges and templates, and test software used for: setup and monitoring of production processes; monitoring of environmental conditions; verification of product conformity; and operations where a defined measurement accuracy is required to assure conformity. QOP-76-01, Measuring and Monitoring Equipment, defines the calibration and control system.

7.1.5.3 Measurement traceability. Where measurement traceability is a requirement, or is considered by High Country to be an essential part of providing confidence in the validity of measurement results, measuring equipment is:

  • Calibrated or verified, or both, at specified intervals or prior to use, against measurement standards traceable to international or national measurement standards; where no such standards exist, the basis used for calibration or verification is retained as documented information;

  • Identified in order to determine its calibration status;

  • Safeguarded from adjustments, damage or deterioration that would invalidate the calibration status and subsequent measurement results.

7.1.5.4 Quality Assurance is responsible for calibrating and maintaining measuring and monitoring devices. All active devices are inventoried in a controlled list indicating calibration status and location. Calibration is recorded in a calibration log and calibrated devices are labeled with a calibration sticker.

7.1.5.5 When measuring equipment is found to be unfit for its intended purpose, High Country determines whether the validity of previous measurement results has been adversely affected and takes appropriate action on the equipment and on any product or service affected. Records of the assessment and of the action taken are retained.

7.1.5.6 Software used in monitoring and measurement of specified requirements is confirmed as fit for the intended application prior to initial use and reconfirmed as necessary. In-house developed inspection, test and monitoring software is validated before use. Commercial software is purchased with validation certificates where available, and is revalidated when the conditions for which it was initially validated are materially changed.

  • The implementation of actions to prevent human error; and

  • The implementation of release, delivery and post-delivery activities.

8.5.1.2 Process validation applies to processes where deficiencies become apparent only after the product is in use or the service has been delivered. Arrangements for such processes include: defined criteria for review and approval of the processes; approval of equipment and qualification of personnel; use of specific methods and procedures; requirements for retained documented information; and revalidation.

8.5.1.3 Actions to prevent human error include mistake-proofing of setups and fixtures, first-article verification, standardized work instructions, checklists for submittal and release, independent checking of critical dimensions and calculations, and competence requirements for critical operations. This is a requirement introduced by ISO 9001:2015 and was not addressed in the previous revision of this manual.

8.5.2Identification and traceability

8.5.2.1 High Country uses suitable means to identify outputs when it is necessary to ensure conformity of products and services.

8.5.2.2 High Country identifies the status of outputs with respect to monitoring and measurement requirements throughout production and service provision. Inspection status identification is applied and maintained in accordance with QOP-75-04.

8.5.2.3 Where traceability is a requirement, High Country controls the unique identification of the outputs and retains the documented information necessary to enable traceability.

8.5.3Property belonging to customers or external providersexpanded in 2015

8.5.3.1 High Country exercises care with property belonging to customers or external providers while it is under the control of High Country or being used by High Country. Such property is identified, verified, protected and safeguarded.

8.5.3.2 Customer property may include materials, components, tools, equipment, premises, intellectual property and personal data. Customer software, documents and other intellectual property are protected to the same extent as High Country internal documents of similar content, unless contractual requirements specify special measures.

8.5.3.3 Customer-supplied products are received and inspected following the same procedure that applies to purchased products (QOP-74-03). Where supplied products fail receiving inspection or are otherwise unsuitable, the customer is contacted.

8.5.3.4 When the property of a customer or external provider is lost, damaged or otherwise found to be unsuitable for use, High Country reports this to the customer or external provider immediately and retains documented information on what has occurred.

8.5.3.5 Where specified in a contract, special handling instructions from a customer take precedence over standard High Country procedures.

8.5.4Preservation

8.5.4.1 High Country preserves the outputs during production and service provision, to the extent necessary to ensure conformity to requirements. Preservation includes identification, handling, contamination control, packaging, storage, transmission or transportation, and protection. Preservation also applies to the constituent parts of a product.

8.5.5Post-delivery activities

8.5.5.1 High Country meets requirements for post-delivery activities associated with the products and services. In determining the extent of post-delivery activities required, High Country considers:

  • Statutory and regulatory requirements;

  • The potential undesired consequences associated with its products and services;

  • The nature, use and intended lifetime of its products and services;

  • Customer requirements; and

  • Customer feedback.

8.5.5.2 Post-delivery activities can include actions under warranty provisions, contractual obligations such as maintenance services, punch-list and remedial work following installation, and supplementary services such as recycling or final disposal. In a limited fashion, High Country provides review procedures to holders of High Country software when a client is experiencing significant difficulty operating the software.

8.5.6Control of changes

8.5.6.1 High Country reviews and controls changes for production or service provision, to the extent necessary to ensure continuing conformity with requirements.

8.5.6.2 Documented information is retained describing the results of the review of changes, the persons authorizing the change, and any necessary actions arising from the review.

8.6Release of Products and Services

8.6.1 High Country implements planned arrangements, at appropriate stages, to verify that product and service requirements have been met. The monitoring and measurement program is defined in drawings and specifications, production work orders, purchasing documents, and inspection and testing procedures. These documents are collectively referred to as control plans.

8.6.2 In-process inspections take the form of first-article inspections, operator and QC inspections, continuous verification by automated inspection equipment, and statistical process control. The focus is on defect prevention rather than detection (QOP-82-03, In-process Inspections).

8.6.3 Final acceptance inspection. Finished products are subject to a final inspection. Inspectors first verify that all specified receiving and in-process inspections have been satisfactorily completed, then perform the remaining inspections and tests necessary to complete the evidence of conformity (QOP-82-04, Final Inspection).

8.6.4 The release of products and services to the customer does not proceed until the planned arrangements have been satisfactorily completed, unless otherwise approved by a relevant authority and, as applicable, by the customer. Only personnel performing final inspections and tests have the authority to release product.

8.6.5 High Country retains documented information on the release of products and services. This includes evidence of conformity with the acceptance criteria and traceability to the person authorizing the release. Filing and retention of inspection records are governed by QOP-75-01 and QOP-42-02.

8.7Control of Nonconforming Outputschanged in 2015

8.7.1 High Country ensures that outputs that do not conform to their requirements are identified and controlled to prevent their unintended use or delivery. This applies to nonconforming products and services detected during or after production, during or after the provision of services, and after delivery.

8.7.2 To prevent unintended use or shipment, nonconforming outputs are marked with a REJECTED label or tag and are segregated where practicable.

8.7.3 High Country takes appropriate action based on the nature of the nonconformity and its effect on the conformity of products and services. Actions may include one or more of the following:

  • Correction — rework or repair;

  • Segregation, containment, return or suspension of provision of products and services;

  • Informing the customer; and

  • Obtaining authorization for acceptance under concession.

8.7.4 Quality Assurance is responsible for reviewing nonconformities and deciding the disposition of nonconforming outputs. In simple and routine cases this responsibility is delegated to production supervisors. Disposition may be to rework, repair, accept as-is under concession, regrade or scrap.

8.7.5 Conformity to requirements is verified when nonconforming outputs are corrected. Reworked products are re-inspected to demonstrate conformity to the original requirements. Repaired and regraded products are inspected to verify that they meet the modified specification (QOP-82-03, QOP-82-04).

8.7.6 Where nonconforming product is detected after delivery or after use has started, the effects or potential effects of the nonconformity are evaluated by Quality Assurance and appropriate action is taken, including notification of the customer and, where applicable, the certification body or regulatory authority.

8.7.7 High Country retains documented information that describes the nonconformity, describes the actions taken, describes any concessions obtained, and identifies the authority deciding the action in respect of the nonconformity. Nonconforming outputs are documented on the Product Nonconformity Report, Form QF-83-01. The use and processing of the PNR are defined in QOP-83-01, Control of Nonconforming Product.

8.7.8 Where a nonconformity indicates a systemic cause, a corrective action is raised in accordance with Section 10.2.

9.0Performance Evaluation

9.1Monitoring, Measurement, Analysis and Evaluation

9.1.1General

9.1.1.1 High Country determines:

  • What needs to be monitored and measured;

  • The methods for monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation needed to ensure valid results;

  • When the monitoring and measuring is to be performed; and

  • When the results from monitoring and measurement are to be analyzed and evaluated.

9.1.1.2 High Country evaluates the performance and the effectiveness of the QMS, and retains appropriate documented information as evidence of the results.

9.1.2Customer satisfaction

9.1.2.1 High Country monitors customer perceptions of the degree to which their needs and expectations have been fulfilled, and determines the methods for obtaining, monitoring and reviewing this information.

9.1.2.2 Information relating to customer satisfaction is collected from the following sources:

  • Customer surveys;

  • Customer feedback on delivered products and services;

  • Customer complaints;

  • Spontaneous expressions of customer satisfaction;

  • Awards and recognition from customers, associations and consumer groups;

  • Warranty and punch-list claims;

  • Repeat business and referrals; and

  • Dealer and agent reports, where applicable.

9.1.2.3 QOP-82-01, Customer Satisfaction, defines the responsibilities and methods for collecting the information. Marketing is responsible for developing suitable indicators of customer satisfaction and for defining methods for collecting and analyzing the pertinent information.

9.1.2.4 Customer satisfaction is used as one of the measures of the performance of the QMS. Customer satisfaction information is reported to, and evaluated by, the management review (Section 9.3 and QOP-56-01).

9.1.3Analysis and evaluation

9.1.3.1 High Country analyzes and evaluates appropriate data and information arising from monitoring and measurement. Data and information recorded in the retained documented information are compiled and analyzed periodically to determine trends in performance and effectiveness and to identify opportunities for improvement.

9.1.3.2 The results of analysis are used to evaluate:

  • Conformity of products and services;

  • The degree of customer satisfaction;

  • The performance and effectiveness of the QMS;

  • Whether planning has been implemented effectively;

  • The effectiveness of actions taken to address risks and opportunities;

  • The performance of external providers; and

  • The need for improvements to the QMS.

9.1.3.3 Categories of information and data compiled and analyzed include:

  • Characteristics of processes and outputs — process performance variation, cycle times, unscheduled machine downtime;

  • Conformity to product and customer requirements — scrap, rework and repair rates, on-time delivery performance;

  • External providers — external provider quality and delivery performance;

  • Customer satisfaction and dissatisfaction — survey results, customer complaints;

  • QMS performance — effectiveness of training, internal audit results, corrective action closure, status of risks and opportunities.

9.1.3.4 Methods of analysis may include statistical techniques. Quality Assurance is responsible for coordinating these activities and for reporting conclusions and trends to top management, normally within the framework of the management review (QOP-56-01).

9.2Internal Audit

9.2.1 High Country conducts internal audits at planned intervals to provide information on whether the QMS:

  • Conforms to the requirements of High Country for its QMS and to the requirements of ISO 9001:2015; and

  • Is effectively implemented and maintained.

9.2.2 High Country:

  • Plans, establishes, implements and maintains an audit program, including the frequency, methods, responsibilities, planning requirements and reporting, which takes into consideration the importance of the processes concerned, changes affecting High Country, and the results of previous audits;

  • Defines the audit criteria and scope for each audit;

  • Selects auditors and conducts audits to ensure objectivity and the impartiality of the audit process — auditors do not audit their own work;

  • Ensures that the results of the audits are reported to relevant management;

  • Takes appropriate correction and corrective action without undue delay; and

  • Retains documented information as evidence of the implementation of the audit program and of the audit results.

9.2.3 The QMS is audited in full over each audit cycle, with the interval for each process determined by its importance and past performance. The frequency for any individual process is not less than once every [12] months.

9.2.4 Appropriate correction and corrective action are taken by the management personnel responsible for the area where nonconforming processes or practices are identified. Auditors follow up to verify that the actions taken are implemented and effective, and report the verification results.

9.2.5 QOP-82-02, Internal Quality Audits, defines the responsibilities and requirements for planning and conducting audits, and for reporting and retaining audit information. ISO 19011 is used as guidance.

9.3Management Reviewexpanded in 2015

9.3.1General

9.3.1.1 Top management reviews the QMS at planned intervals to ensure its continuing suitability, adequacy, effectiveness and alignment with the strategic direction of High Country.

9.3.1.2 Management reviews are conducted at least once each year. More frequent reviews are scheduled in periods when organizational, technological, product or other changes require increased attention and input from top management.

9.3.1.3 Management reviews are chaired by the CEO or COO and are attended by the Division Directors and the Quality Assurance Manager. The processes for initiating and conducting management reviews and for documenting their conclusions are defined in QOP-56-01, Management Review.

9.3.1.4 In addition, the Quality Assurance Manager reports to the CEO or COO monthly, preferably at the monthly management meeting, on any significant deficiency requiring immediate process improvement. The Quality Assurance Manager and the internal auditors meet as required to review quality issues arising from internal audits or from responses to nonconformities.

9.3.2Management review inputs

9.3.2.1 The management review is planned and carried out taking into consideration:

  • The status of actions from previous management reviews;

  • Changes in external and internal issues that are relevant to the QMS (Section 4.1);

  • Changes in the needs and expectations of interested parties (Section 4.2);

  • Information on the performance and effectiveness of the QMS, including trends in: customer satisfaction and feedback from relevant interested parties; the extent to which quality objectives have been met; process performance and conformity of products and services; nonconformities and corrective actions; monitoring and measurement results; audit results; and the performance of external providers;

  • The adequacy of resources;

  • The effectiveness of actions taken to address risks and opportunities (Section 6.1); and

  • Opportunities for improvement.

9.3.2.2 Additional inputs considered at High Country include new or revised statutory and regulatory requirements, changes to AWI, AIA and UL standards, the status of organizational knowledge and knowledge at risk (Section 7.1.6), and the status of any determination of non-applicability under Section 1.3.

9.3.3Management review outputs

9.3.3.1 The outputs of the management review include decisions and actions related to:

  • Opportunities for improvement;

  • Any need for changes to the QMS; and

  • Resource needs.

9.3.3.2 Management reviews conclude with the setting of new quality objectives and the initiation of actions to improve the QMS, its processes, and its products and services.

9.3.3.3 High Country retains documented information as evidence of the results of management reviews. Results are documented in the minutes of the review meeting, which record improvement actions and assign responsibilities and resources for their implementation.

9.3.3.4 Improvements are communicated to all High Country personnel in accordance with Section 7.4, and to specific customers as necessary. Division Directors hold annual meetings with their Managers and affected personnel following the management review — documenting topics and attendance — to communicate the importance of maintaining the quality process, implement specific recommendations from the review, and assign action items.

10.0Improvement

10.1General

10.1.1 High Country determines and selects opportunities for improvement and implements any necessary actions to meet customer requirements and enhance customer satisfaction.

10.1.2 These include:

  • Improving products and services to meet requirements as well as to address future needs and expectations;

  • Correcting, preventing or reducing undesired effects; and

  • Improving the performance and effectiveness of the QMS.

10.1.3 Improvement can include correction, corrective action, continual improvement, breakthrough change, innovation and reorganization.

10.2Nonconformity and Corrective Actionchanged in 2015

10.2.1 When a nonconformity occurs, including any arising from complaints, High Country:

  • Reacts to the nonconformity and, as applicable, takes action to control and correct it, and deals with the consequences;

  • Evaluates the need for action to eliminate the cause of the nonconformity, in order that it does not recur or occur elsewhere, by reviewing and analyzing the nonconformity, determining the causes of the nonconformity, and determining whether similar nonconformities exist or could potentially occur;

  • Implements any action needed;

  • Reviews the effectiveness of any corrective action taken;

  • Updates risks and opportunities determined during planning, if necessary (Section 6.1); and

  • Makes changes to the QMS, if necessary.

10.2.2 Corrective actions are appropriate to the effects of the nonconformities encountered.

10.2.3 High Country retains documented information as evidence of the nature of the nonconformities and any subsequent actions taken, and of the results of any corrective action.

10.2.4 The process for requesting, implementing and verifying corrective action is defined in QOP-85-03. ISO 9001:2015 no longer contains a separate requirement for preventive action; the intent of preventive action is now achieved through the risk-based thinking described in Section 6.1. Where a corrective action review identifies that a similar nonconformity could occur elsewhere, the risk register is updated accordingly.

10.2.5 Customer complaints. Complaints that allege deficiencies related to the identity, quality, durability, reliability, safety, effectiveness or performance of a product or service are logged and documented. Complaints that involve a possible failure of a product, labeling or packaging to meet any of its specifications are always investigated, and the results of the investigation are documented. The system for receiving, logging, investigating and responding to customer complaints is defined in QOP-85-02, Customer Feedback and Complaints.

10.3Continual Improvement

10.3.1 High Country continually improves the suitability, adequacy and effectiveness of the QMS.

10.3.2 High Country considers the results of analysis and evaluation (Section 9.1.3) and the outputs from management review (Section 9.3) to determine whether there are needs or opportunities that shall be addressed as part of continual improvement.

10.3.3 Continual improvement is driven through the use of the quality policy, quality objectives, audit results, analysis of data, corrective action, risk and opportunity actions, and management review. QOP-85-01, Continual Improvement, defines this process.

10.3.4 Internal audit results and quality performance data are analyzed at the management review to assess the effectiveness of the QMS and current organizational performance. Opportunities and priorities for improvement are identified by comparing present quality performance to the goals and aspirations defined in the quality policy.

10.3.5 Improvement projects are defined either as corrective actions or as quality objectives, in accordance with QOP-85-03 and QOP-56-01 respectively.
