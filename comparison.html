The Silo Problem

Why Adding Another Point Tool Doesn't Fix Your Operation

Most make-to-order shops run the same project through 3 or 4 disconnected software packages. Information is re-entered manually into each one, introducing massive operational drag.

Email & Attachments

"Where critical project data goes to die"

Estimates, spec revisions, and approvals get buried in inbox threads. When critical job decisions live inside PDF attachments, your shop is always one outdated revision away from a $10k mistake.

Fatal Flaw: Zero revision control
Excel

"Version chaos, no shared source of truth"

Estimates, cost breakdowns, and schedules live in files emailed back and forth — the "final_v3_ACTUAL.xlsx" problem, multiplied across every active job in the shop.

Fatal Flaw: Fragile disconnected logic
Monday.com

"Tracks tasks, not the operation"

Great for a high-level status board. It has no idea what's in your price book, can't generate a proposal from real spec data, and can't produce an AIA billing application.

Fatal Flaw: No shop domain logic
Smartsheet

"A better spreadsheet is still a spreadsheet"

Grids and automations help, but production tracking, shop-floor time, and margin reporting still live somewhere else entirely — usually back in manual Excel sheets.

Fatal Flaw: No CNC / Nesting bridge
Procore

"Built for the GC's field, not your shop floor"

Strong at jobsite submittals and RFIs on the GC side. But it carries no tiered price book, Bill of Operations, cut-list engine, or shop floor kiosk — because that's not its job.

Fatal Flaw: Ignores manufacturing

The Solution Isn't A Fourth Task Board. It's One Unified Platform.

Project Center 2.0 understands what a make-to-order bid, spec revision, cut list, submittal package, and AIA G702 billing application actually are — because it was built specifically for the way shop operations run.
