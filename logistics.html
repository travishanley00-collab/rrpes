Logistics & Multi-Site Planner

Find the best shop for the job. Then schedule it there.

Every job has a manufacturing site, a delivery destination, and a schedule — and when you run more than one shop, the "which site" question is really a freight-and-capacity question in disguise. This module scores each candidate site against the job's destination and every shop's current load, so the site that wins is the one that's actually cheapest and fastest to ship from and has room on the floor — not just whichever one is closest to headquarters.

What the module does

Site selection, freight, and scheduling — together

These aren't three separate spreadsheets. Picking a site changes the freight estimate, which changes the job cost, which changes whether that site's schedule can absorb the job.

FIT

Best-Fit Site Scoring

For a given job destination, every eligible manufacturing site is scored on freight distance and cost, current capacity, crew skill match, and material availability — ranked, not guessed at.

FRT

Freight & Landed-Cost Modeling

Distance-based freight and delivery-mode estimates (LTL, FTL, company truck, or crew-carried) per lane, so the "cheapest site to build at" and "cheapest site to ship from" get reconciled before you commit.

CAP

Capacity-Aware Multi-Site Scheduling

One production calendar across every shop — see what percentage of each site's near-term capacity is already booked before assigning a new job there, not after.

LANE

Standing Freight Lanes

Recurring routes between a shop and a job cluster (a metro area, a GC's regional footprint) can be saved as a lane with a known rate, so repeat business skips the estimate step entirely.

JC

Job Costing Tie-In

Whichever site and freight lane get selected for a job, the actual freight and site-labor cost post straight to that job's cost record in FinanceCenter — no separate reconciliation later.

RE

Re-Score on Change

A shop goes over capacity, a freight rate changes, or a job's destination moves — re-run the fit score and see whether a different site now wins, before material gets cut at the wrong one.

How it works

From a new job to an assigned shop and route

1

Job destination and requirements come in

Delivery address, target install date, and any crew/skill or material requirements attach to the job as soon as it's won — the same job record FinanceCenter and the material requisition workflow already use.

2

Every eligible site gets scored

Freight distance and mode, current booked capacity for the target week, and material/crew fit are weighed together into one fit score per site — ties broken in favor of the site with more open capacity.

3

The best-fit site (and a runner-up) is presented

The recommendation shows its reasoning — freight estimate, capacity load, and score breakdown — so a scheduler can accept it or override it with a documented reason.

4

The job lands on that site's production calendar

Assigning the job books capacity at the chosen site immediately, so the next job's fit score sees the updated load — no two jobs get double-booked into the same week by two different estimators.

5

Freight and site cost post to the job automatically

The freight estimate — and the actual carrier cost once shipped — write straight to the job's cost record, so the delivered job cost reflects exactly which shop built it and how it got there.
