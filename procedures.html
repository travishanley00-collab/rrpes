How This Manual Is Organized

This Operational Procedures Manual is the second-level document of the Rock River Consulting Quality Management System. It sits beneath the Quality Management System Manual (QMS-00), which defines the QMS at the level of the ten ISO 9001:2015 clauses, and above the individual work instructions and forms used at the point of work.

Each procedure in this manual is numbered QOP-SS-NN, where SS is the clause of QMS-00 the procedure principally supports and NN is the sequence number of the procedure within that clause — the same numbering convention introduced in Section 2.2 and Appendix C of QMS-00. Each procedure states its Purpose, Scope, Responsibilities, step-by-step Procedure, the Records it generates, and the Forms (QF series) it uses, and links back to the corresponding section of QMS-00.

The two documents are maintained together: a revision to a clause of QMS-00 that changes a control is reflected in the corresponding QOP at the next revision, and a change in practice discovered while executing a QOP is brought back to QMS-00 through the corrective action and management review processes (QOP-85-03 and QOP-56-01).

DocumentLevel and Purpose
QMS-00, Quality Management System ManualTop-level document. Defines the QMS to the structure of ISO 9001:2015 Clauses 1–10: scope, context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation and improvement.
QOP-00, Operational Procedures Manual (this document)Second-level document. Defines, for each QMS process, the responsible roles and the step-by-step method used to carry it out and to generate objective evidence of conformity.
Work instructions (WI) and Quality Forms (QF)Third-level documents. Guide a specific task (a setup, an inspection, a calibration) or provide the template for a specific record.

QOP-42-01Control of Documents

Supports Clause(s)Process OwnerRevisionCompanion Manual Section
7.5.2, 7.5.3Quality Assurance ManagerRev. Aquality.html § 7.5.2

Purpose

To ensure that documented information required by the Quality Management System — this manual, operational procedures, work instructions, drawings, specifications, and external standards and codes — is reviewed, approved, identified, distributed, and kept current before it is used, and that obsolete documents are prevented from unintended use.

Scope

Applies to all controlled documents of the QMS, including this manual, the QOP series, QF-series forms, work instructions, engineering drawings and specifications, and documents of external origin (AWI, AIA and UL standards, building codes, customer specifications) that are relied upon in performing work.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
Quality Assurance ManagerMaintains the master list of controlled documents (Form QF-42-01-1); administers the review, approval, revision and withdrawal of controlled documents; verifies annually that external standards in use are current editions.
Document originator / process ownerDrafts and proposes revisions to documents within their process area; routes drafts for review.
Division Directors and ManagersReview and approve documents affecting their area prior to release; ensure current revisions are in use at points of use.
All personnelUse only current, controlled copies; report suspected obsolete or illegible documents to the Quality Assurance Manager.

Procedure

1. Identification of documents

Each controlled document is assigned a unique identifier consistent with the numbering convention of Appendix C (QOP-SS-NN for operational procedures; QF-SS-NN-M for forms), a title, a revision level, and an effective date.

2. Drafting and review

  • A new or revised document is drafted by the responsible process owner and circulated to the functions it affects for review and comment.

  • Comments are resolved before the document is submitted for approval. Where a document affects product conformity or a customer commitment, the Quality Assurance Manager is included in the review.

3. Approval and release

  • Documents are approved by the process owner's Manager or Division Director and, for this manual and QOP-series procedures, by the Quality Assurance Manager, prior to release.

  • Approval is recorded on the document itself (signature block or approval log) and on the master list.

4. Distribution and access

  • Current controlled documents are made available electronically through the document management system, and in printed form only where stamped “CONTROLLED COPY.” Uncontrolled printed copies bear the notice “For information only — not a controlled document if printed.”

  • The Quality Assurance Manager ensures documents are available at the point of use, including job sites, where applicable.

5. Revision

  • Revisions are numbered sequentially (Rev. A, B, C …) and re-approved through the same review and approval process as the original document. A revision summary is recorded in the document's change history.

  • Revised documents supersede and replace the prior revision at all points of use upon release.

6. Control of obsolete documents

  • Obsolete controlled documents are removed from points of use or are clearly marked “OBSOLETE” if retained for reference (for example, in project or legal records).

  • The Quality Assurance Manager retains one marked copy of each superseded revision for the retention period defined in QOP-42-02.

7. Documents of external origin

  • AWI, AIA and UL standards, building codes, and customer-supplied drawings and specifications are logged on the master list with their edition or issue date. The Quality Assurance Manager verifies at least annually, and whenever a project requires it, that the edition in use is current.

  • Superseded external standards are withdrawn from points of use in the same manner as internal documents.

QOP-42-02Control of Records

Supports Clause(s)Process OwnerRevisionCompanion Manual Section
7.5.3Quality Assurance ManagerRev. Aquality.html § 7.5.3

Purpose

To ensure that documented information retained as evidence of conformity and of the effective operation of the QMS is identified, stored, protected, retrievable, retained for a defined period, and dispositioned in a controlled manner.

Scope

Applies to all quality records generated by QMS processes, including project deliverable documentation, inspection and test records, training records, audit records, management review minutes, calibration records, nonconformity reports, corrective action records, and supplier evaluation records.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
Quality Assurance ManagerMaintains the Records Retention Schedule; audits record-keeping practice during internal audits.
Record originator (each Manager / department)Identifies, protects, and files records generated within their function in accordance with the retention schedule.
Division DirectorsEnsure storage conditions in their division protect records from loss, damage and unauthorized alteration.

Procedure

1. Identification and filing

Records are identified by document/form number, project or work order number, and date. Records are filed by category — project files, QMS master records, training files, calibration files, subcontractor and external provider records, annual quality assessments — as defined in the Records Retention Schedule.

2. Storage and protection

Paper records are stored in facilities that protect against fire, moisture and unauthorized access; electronic records are stored on the company's managed servers, which are backed up on a defined schedule. Access to records is restricted to personnel who need it to perform their duties.

3. Retention

The Records Retention Schedule specifies, for each category of record, the retention period and the responsible custodian. Retention periods take into account statutory, contractual (including AWI and UL follow-up service) and warranty requirements.

4. Retrieval

Records are indexed so that any record can be retrieved within one business day. Records supporting an active nonconformity, audit finding or customer claim are retrieved and made available on request without delay.

5. Disposition

At the end of the retention period, records are reviewed by the Quality Assurance Manager and either destroyed by secure means or retained further if subject to legal hold, open warranty, or ongoing litigation. Disposition is logged.

QOP-56-01Management Review

Supports Clause(s)Process OwnerRevisionCompanion Manual Section
6.2, 6.3, 9.3Chief Executive Officer / Chief Operating OfficerRev. Aquality.html § 9.3

Purpose

To provide top management with a structured, periodic review of the QMS sufficient to ensure its continuing suitability, adequacy, effectiveness and alignment with the strategic direction of Rock River Consulting, and to establish and monitor quality objectives and planned changes to the QMS.

Scope

Applies to the annual (or more frequent) management review meeting and to the establishment, communication and monitoring of quality objectives throughout the year.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
CEO / COOChairs the management review; approves the QMS scope, quality policy, quality objectives, and any determination of non-applicability.
Quality Assurance ManagerSchedules the review; compiles and presents the review inputs; records and distributes the minutes; tracks action items to closure.
Division DirectorsAttend the review; report performance and resource needs for their division; hold divisional communication meetings following the review.

Procedure

1. Scheduling

Management review is conducted at least annually. Additional reviews are scheduled when significant organizational, technological, product, regulatory or market changes warrant more frequent top-management attention.

2. Inputs

The Quality Assurance Manager compiles, for presentation at the review: status of actions from prior reviews; changes in external/internal issues (Section 4.1) and interested-party requirements (Section 4.2); QMS performance data — customer satisfaction and feedback, quality objective attainment, process performance and product conformity, nonconformities and corrective actions, monitoring and measurement results, audit results, and external provider performance; adequacy of resources; effectiveness of actions on risks and opportunities (Section 6.1); status of organizational knowledge and knowledge at risk; and opportunities for improvement.

3. Conduct of the review

The CEO or COO chairs the meeting. Each input is presented and discussed with the Division Directors and Quality Assurance Manager. Decisions are made in real time where possible.

4. Outputs

The review concludes with: decisions and actions on opportunities for improvement; any needed changes to the QMS (planned in accordance with Section 6.3); resource decisions; and new or revised quality objectives for the coming period, each assigned an owner, a target, and a target date.

5. Recording and communication

The Quality Assurance Manager records the review in meeting minutes within ten business days, distributes the minutes and action items to attendees, and updates the Quality Objectives Record (Form QF-56-01-1). Division Directors hold a divisional communication meeting, documenting topics and attendance, within 30 days of the review to communicate outcomes and assign related action items.

6. Follow-up

The Quality Assurance Manager tracks open action items between reviews and reports status at the next review as a standing input.

QOP-62-01Competence, Awareness and Training

Supports Clause(s)Process OwnerRevisionCompanion Manual Section
7.2, 7.3Human Resources, with Managers and the Quality Assurance ManagerRev. Aquality.html § 7.2

Purpose

To ensure that persons doing work under the control of Rock River Consulting are competent on the basis of appropriate education, training or experience, and are aware of the quality policy, relevant quality objectives, their contribution to the QMS, and the implications of nonconformity.

Scope

Applies to all employees, and to temporary staff and subcontractor personnel performing work under Rock River Consulting's control, including general orientation, position-specific training, technical certification, and internal auditor training.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
Human ResourcesAdministers company-wide orientation and general training programs; maintains the master training record system.
ManagersIdentify competency requirements and training needs within their division; assign qualified personnel, including Technical Associates; evaluate training effectiveness.
Quality Assurance ManagerIdentifies quality-related training needs and frequency; ensures internal auditors are trained per QOP-82-02.
Division DirectorsDesignate internal auditors and ensure competence of assigned personnel.

Procedure

1. Determining competence requirements

For each position affecting the performance or effectiveness of the QMS, the responsible Manager defines the required education, training, technical certification, and experience, recorded on a Job Description (Form QF-62-01-2) or competence matrix.

2. Identifying and closing gaps

Managers compare current personnel qualifications to requirements and identify training needs. Actions to close a gap may include training, mentoring, reassignment, or hiring/contracting competent persons.

3. General orientation

All new personnel, including subcontractor and temporary personnel working at Rock River Consulting facilities or on Rock River Consulting projects, receive orientation covering the quality policy, applicable quality objectives, safety requirements, and the implications of nonconformity, before beginning unsupervised work.

4. Position and technical training

Managers provide or arrange training on equipment, processes, inspection and test methods, and analytical techniques relevant to the position. Where a customer contract specifies a technical certification, Managers ensure the individual holds and maintains it, and file evidence in the project records.

5. Evaluating effectiveness

Where practicable, the effectiveness of training is evaluated through on-the-job observation, testing, or performance monitoring, and the result is recorded on the Employee Training Record (Form QF-62-01-3).

6. Awareness

Awareness of the quality policy and current quality objectives is reinforced through posting, departmental meetings, toolbox talks, and communication of management review outcomes (Section 7.4).

7. Records

Training records, certifications, and job descriptions are retained in each employee's training file and in the division's technical personnel files.

QOP-63-01Equipment

Supports Clause(s)Process OwnerRevisionCompanion Manual Section
7.1.3Departmental Managers, with the IT Manager for information systemsRev. Aquality.html § 7.1.3

Purpose

To ensure that production equipment, machines, hardware, software and supporting infrastructure are maintained in a condition suitable to achieve conformity of products and services.

Scope

Applies to woodworking and finishing machinery, material handling equipment, hand and power tools, production software (CAD/CAM, CNC programming, scheduling), and information technology infrastructure.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
Departmental ManagerIdentifies infrastructure and equipment needs; schedules and documents preventive maintenance; requests repair or replacement of unsuitable equipment.
Equipment operatorsPerform pre-use checks; report malfunctions or damage immediately; do not operate equipment known to be out of adjustment.
IT ManagerMaintains information and communication technology infrastructure and selects and administers IT service contracts.

Procedure

1. Identifying equipment needs

Departmental Managers identify the need for new equipment or for modification or repair of existing equipment and submit requests to top management for review and approval.

2. Preventive maintenance

Production equipment is maintained on a schedule specified by the manufacturer or by the responsible Manager. Maintenance activities and dates are logged against each piece of equipment.

3. Corrective maintenance

Equipment found to be out of adjustment or malfunctioning is tagged out of service and is not used for production until repaired and verified suitable. Where the malfunction may have affected product already produced, the affected output is evaluated under QOP-83-01.

4. Software

In-house developed software used for production or design (including CNC programs and cut-list/optimization software) is verified before initial use and revalidated when materially changed. Commercial software is maintained under vendor support and license agreements administered by the IT Manager.

5. Facilities

Maintenance of buildings and facilities — lighting, HVAC, landscaping, cleaning — is performed by external contractors on a scheduled basis and controlled as an externally provided service under QOP-74-01.

To assign responsibility and establish the framework for planning the processes needed to meet the requirements for a project's products and services, consistent with the criteria and acceptance requirements of the contract.

QOP-72-01Order Processing and Review

Supports Clause(s)Process OwnerRevisionCompanion Manual Section
8.2.1–8.2.4Project Manager / SalesRev. Aquality.html § 8.2

Purpose

To ensure that customer requirements — including delivery, statutory, regulatory and unstated but necessary requirements — are determined, are reviewed for the ability of Rock River Consulting to meet them prior to commitment, and are consistently communicated and controlled through any subsequent change.

Scope

Applies to all inquiries, bids, contracts, purchase orders and verbal orders accepted by Rock River Consulting, and to change orders and amendments arising during a project.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
Sales / Project ManagerDetermines and reviews product and service requirements prior to commitment; resolves differing requirements; confirms verbal orders.
Quality Assurance ManagerSupports review of unfamiliar specification, code or AWI grade requirements; supports risk assessment of the order (Section 6.1).

Procedure

1. Determining requirements

Any documentation from the customer that authorizes work is treated as the customer's contract for purposes of this procedure. Requirements determined include those stated by the customer (including delivery and post-delivery activities), requirements necessary for intended use though not stated, applicable statutory/regulatory and AWI/UL requirements, and additional requirements Rock River Consulting determines are necessary.

2. Review prior to commitment

Before committing to supply, the order is reviewed to confirm requirements are defined, that differences between the order and any prior quotation are resolved, and that Rock River Consulting has the capacity, schedule and material availability to meet the order. Risks and opportunities identified during the review (capacity, schedule, unfamiliar specification, material lead time) are logged in accordance with Section 6.1.

3. Verbal orders

Where the customer provides no documented statement of requirements, the requirements are confirmed in writing before acceptance.

4. Change orders

Change orders and amendments are processed and reviewed using the same procedure as initial orders and are communicated to all functions that may be affected. Requests for information during construction are handled per the AIA RFI process.

5. Records

The results of the review, and any new or changed requirements, are retained in the project file.

QOP-73-01Design Control

Supports Clause(s)Process OwnerRevisionCompanion Manual Section
8.3Engineering, Design Project Manager, and the Quality Assurance ManagerRev. Aquality.html § 8.3

Purpose

To plan, execute and control the design and development of customer-specified products and modifications so that design output meets design input and the resulting product is suitable for its intended use.

Scope

Applies whenever Rock River Consulting designs or modifies a customer-specified product under contract, from planning through release of design output for production and through any subsequent design change.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
ManagerSchedules the project, assigns qualified personnel, and controls organizational and technical interfaces.
Design Project ManagerPlans design activities (Form QF-73-01-1); identifies design, review, verification and validation activities; updates the plan as design progresses.
Quality Assurance ManagerChecks and approves design output prior to release for production.

Procedure

1. Planning

The Design Project Manager documents the design project plan (Form QF-73-01-1), considering the nature, duration and complexity of the work; required review, verification and validation stages; responsibilities and authorities; resource needs; interfaces between functions; and the level of customer involvement expected.

2. Inputs

Design inputs — functional and performance requirements, applicable statutory/regulatory and building code requirements, UL listing requirements, AWI and AIA standards, information from previous similar designs, and the potential consequences of failure — are reviewed for adequacy, completeness and freedom from conflict before use. Inputs are retained in the design project file.

3. Controls, reviews, verification and validation

Design reviews are held at planned stages with representatives of the functions concerned, to evaluate the ability of the design to meet requirements and to identify needed actions. Verification confirms design output meets design input; validation confirms the resulting product meets the requirements for its specified application or intended use, completed prior to delivery wherever practical. In-house software used for design or critical calculations is reviewed and approved before use.

4. Outputs

Design output — drawings, cut bills, samples, models, math data and software specifying manufacture, packaging, labeling, installation and servicing, together with acceptance criteria — meets input requirements, is adequate for downstream processes, and specifies characteristics essential to safe and proper provision. Engineering design and modification documents include manufacture, layout, assembly, piping/isometric, and installation drawings, and UL point-to-point wiring/cabling diagrams as applicable.

5. Release

Design output is checked and approved by the Quality Assurance Manager before release for production, and is then controlled under QOP-42-01.

6. Changes

Design changes are identified, reviewed (including their effect on constituent parts and product already delivered), verified and validated as appropriate, and approved before implementation. Records of changes, reviews, authorization and any actions to prevent adverse impact are retained.

QOP-74-01Supplier Evaluation and Monitoring

Supports Clause(s)Process OwnerRevisionCompanion Manual Section
8.4.1Purchasing, with Quality AssuranceRev. Aquality.html § 8.4.1

Purpose

To evaluate, select, monitor and re-evaluate external providers based on their ability to supply processes, products and services conforming to Rock River Consulting's requirements, and to maintain the Approved Supplier List.

Scope

Applies to all external providers of materials, components, subcontracted labor, and outsourced services whose output is incorporated into, or provided on behalf of, Rock River Consulting's products and services.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
PurchasingMaintains the Approved Supplier List; conducts initial evaluation and periodic re-evaluation; issues corrective action requests to underperforming external providers.
Quality AssuranceEstablishes evaluation criteria appropriate to the risk posed by the process, product or service; reviews evaluation results.

Procedure

1. Initial evaluation

A prospective external provider is evaluated against criteria derived from specifications, client requirements and contract requirements — which may include quality system evidence, references, sample or trial orders, and, for critical processes, a supplier audit (Form QF-74-01-1). Evaluation results are recorded (Form QF-74-01-2).

2. Approval and listing

Approved external providers are added to the Approved Supplier List. Orders for materials, components and subcontracted services are placed only with listed external providers, except where Purchasing documents and justifies a one-time exception.

3. Ongoing monitoring

External provider performance is monitored on an ongoing basis using receiving inspection results (QOP-74-03), on-time delivery performance, and nonconformity history (QOP-83-01).

4. Re-evaluation

Each listed external provider is formally re-evaluated at a frequency determined by the risk of the process, product or service supplied, and always following a significant quality failure.

5. Corrective action and removal

Recurring or significant nonconformance is addressed with the external provider under QOP-85-03. An external provider that fails to sustain acceptable performance is placed on probationary status or removed from the Approved Supplier List.

QOP-74-02Purchasing

Supports Clause(s)Process OwnerRevisionCompanion Manual Section
8.4.3Purchasing AgentRev. Aquality.html § 8.4.3

Purpose

To ensure that purchasing documents clearly and completely describe the products, services or processes ordered, including quality requirements, and are reviewed and approved before release to the external provider.

Scope

Applies to purchase orders and subcontract agreements for materials, components, and services placed with external providers on the Approved Supplier List.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
Purchasing AgentPrepares, reviews and releases purchasing documents; is the only individual authorized to sign a purchase contract, in accordance with the Rock River Consulting Procedure Manual for Purchases.
CEO / COO / CFO / Contracts ManagerAuthorized to execute contracts within their delegated authority.
Operations DirectorDesignates an acting Purchasing Agent in the absence of the Purchasing Agent.

Procedure

1. Determining purchasing information

Purchasing information describes the product, service or process to be purchased, including, as appropriate, requirements for approval of product, process and equipment; qualification of personnel; and applicable QMS requirements. The Purchasing Agent verifies the adequacy of requirements before they are communicated to the external provider.

2. Preparing purchasing documents

Purchasing documents precisely identify the product or service, applicable specifications, revision level, quantity, delivery requirements, and any required certifications (mill certs, UL listing, grade certification). For job-related materials, contractual quality requirements applicable to Rock River Consulting are flowed down to the external provider.

3. Review and release

Purchasing documents are reviewed and approved for adequacy prior to release. General office supplies, including computing materials, are procured through the normal competitive process without a formal purchasing document.

4. Verification arrangements

Where Rock River Consulting or its customer intends to verify product at the external provider's premises, the purchasing document states the intended verification arrangements and the method of product release.

QOP-74-03Verification of Purchased Product

Supports Clause(s)Process OwnerRevisionCompanion Manual Section
8.4.4Quality Assurance / ReceivingRev. Aquality.html § 8.4.4

Purpose

To verify that purchased products meet specified purchase requirements before use in production or dispatch to customers.

Scope

Applies to all materials, components and subcontracted work received from external providers, including customer-supplied product received for incorporation into the work.

Responsibilities

QOP-76-01Measuring and Monitoring Equipment

Supports Clause(s)Process OwnerRevisionCompanion Manual Section
7.1.5Quality AssuranceRev. Aquality.html § 7.1.5

Purpose

To ensure that measuring and monitoring equipment used to verify conformity of products and services provides valid and reliable results, and is calibrated, identified, safeguarded and controlled.

Scope

Applies to measuring and test equipment, comparative reference hardware (gauges, templates), and test/measurement software used for production setup and monitoring, environmental monitoring, and verification of product conformity.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility

Software used for monitoring and measurement is confirmed fit for the intended application before initial use and reconfirmed after any material change. In-house developed software is validated before use; commercial software is used within its vendor validation, where available.

Purpose

To identify, document, segregate and disposition outputs that do not conform to their requirements, to prevent their unintended use or delivery, and to evaluate and act on nonconformity discovered after delivery.

Scope

Applies to nonconforming raw material, work-in-process, finished product, and installed work, however and whenever the nonconformity is detected.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
Any employeeIdentifies and reports a suspected nonconformity immediately.

A nonconforming output is documented on a Product Nonconformity Report (Form QF-83-01) and is marked with a REJECTED label or tag and segregated where practicable to prevent unintended use or delivery.

The PNR records the nature of the nonconformity, the actions taken, any concessions obtained, and the authority deciding the disposition.

6. Systemic cause

Where a nonconformity indicates a systemic cause, a corrective action is raised under QOP-85-03.

QOP-85-03Corrective Action

Supports Clause(s)Process OwnerRevisionCompanion Manual Section
10.2; preventive intent now addressed under 6.1Quality Assurance ManagerRev. Aquality.html § 10.2

Purpose

To eliminate the cause of a nonconformity so that it does not recur or occur elsewhere, through a structured process of correction, cause analysis, action, verification, and update of the risk register where warranted.

Scope

Applies to nonconformities arising from any source — internal audit, customer complaint, PNR, process monitoring, or external audit. Risk-based thinking under Section 6.1 addresses the intent formerly met by a separate preventive action clause; where a corrective action review identifies that a similar nonconformity could occur elsewhere, the risk register is updated accordingly.

Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
OriginatorRaises a Corrective Action Request (Form QF-85-04-1) when a nonconformity, audit finding, or significant complaint is identified.
Responsible ManagerInvestigates cause, determines and implements corrective action, and reports completion.
Quality Assurance ManagerReviews the adequacy of proposed actions, verifies effectiveness, updates the risk and opportunity register where indicated, and tracks requests to closure.

Procedure

1. Initiation

A Corrective Action Request is raised for a nonconformity, an audit finding, a significant or recurring customer complaint, or a systemic issue identified through data analysis (Section 9.1.3).

2. Reaction

The immediate condition is controlled and corrected, and its consequences are addressed, before root-cause analysis is completed.

3. Cause analysis

The responsible Manager evaluates the need for action to eliminate the cause, reviewing and analyzing the nonconformity, determining its cause(s), and determining whether similar nonconformities exist or could occur elsewhere.

4. Action

Corrective action appropriate to the effects of the nonconformity encountered is determined and implemented, including, where necessary, changes to the QMS, work instructions, or training.

5. Verification of effectiveness

The Quality Assurance Manager reviews the effectiveness of the action taken, typically at the next relevant audit cycle or a defined follow-up interval.

6. Update of risk register

Where the review indicates the nonconformity could recur or occur elsewhere, the Risk and Opportunity Register (Section 6.1) is updated with the associated risk and any additional preventive measure.

7. Closure

The request is closed by the Quality Assurance Manager once effectiveness is verified and evidence is retained.
